# UX Ideas: Efteling Family Fast-Scan Flow — 2026-08-25

## 1. Family Bundle with Moment Tabs

**Pitch:** Let users create a single “day bundle” for outings like Efteling, then drop multiple passes inside it: three entry tickets, one parking ticket, maybe even a meal voucher later. Inside the bundle, the app splits everything by **moment** instead of by storage structure: **Enter Park**, **Later Today**, **Exit Parking**. The user never has to remember where a code lives; they just open the bundle and tap the moment they are in.

**Problem it solves:** Families think in real-world moments, not in separate pass records scattered across a list.

## 2. Turnstile Relay Mode

**Pitch:** Turn a ticket bundle into a full-screen relay: one giant code on screen, a big “next ticket” thumb target, and a calm progress marker like **1 of 3**, **2 of 3**, **3 of 3**. After each scan, the next family ticket slides in automatically or with a single flick, so the parent can keep one hand on the phone and one hand on the child. It should feel less like browsing passes and more like feeding a smooth ticket conveyor belt into the gate.

**Problem it solves:** Scanning several nearly identical tickets in a row is stressful and slow when each one must be found manually.

## 3. Assigned-to-a-Person Tickets

**Pitch:** Inside a grouped pass, each code can be assigned to a person with a tiny identity cue: “Mama,” “Papa,” “Mila,” “Noah,” plus a color, emoji, or silhouette. In relay mode, those identities stay visible at the top so the parent always knows whose ticket is currently on screen. After a scan, that person’s ticket visually “checks off” and drops to the back, which makes the sequence feel controlled instead of chaotic.

**Problem it solves:** When multiple entry tickets look the same, users lose confidence about which one has already been scanned and whose turn is next.

## 4. Parking Panic Button

**Pitch:** Let any pass be marked as a **panic pass** for high-pressure moments, then make it retrievable from absurdly fast entry points: a long-press on the app icon, a lock-screen shortcut, a persistent notification on outing day, or a huge “Show parking now” chip inside the bundle. This is not a favorite star; it is an emergency lane. The parking code should appear in one motion, with zero list browsing and zero risk of opening the wrong ticket while cars pile up behind you.

**Problem it solves:** The parking ticket matters at one very specific, high-stress moment where even a few extra taps feel terrible.

## 5. Trip Timeline

**Pitch:** Reframe a grouped outing as a mini timeline: **Arrive → Enter Park → Ride/Shop Extras → Leave Parking**. Each stage reveals only the passes that matter right now and quietly hides the rest until later. That makes PassDrop feel like a day-of companion instead of a storage cabinet, and it gives bundles a story arc: “here’s what you need now” instead of “here’s everything you own.”

**Problem it solves:** Users freeze when too many equally important-looking codes are shown at once under pressure.

## 6. Backseat Hand-Off View

**Pitch:** Add a special “passenger mode” for grouped outings: the phone can stay in the driver’s hand, but the screen mirrors an oversized, ultra-high-contrast code with left/right tap zones so a passenger can advance tickets without learning the app. For the parking ticket, the driver gets a minimal single-code view with extra brightness and no surrounding chrome. It turns a stressful shared moment into a coordinated tiny ritual: one person drives, one person taps, nobody debates where the code is.

**Problem it solves:** Family travel often involves two adults sharing the retrieval job in awkward physical conditions, especially in a car queue.

## 7. Used/Not-Yet-Used Ticket States

**Pitch:** Give grouped tickets a strong live state change during fast entry: **ready**, **just scanned**, **already used**, **skip for now**. After each scan, the completed ticket collapses into a reassuring thumbnail while the next one takes center stage. If a staff member says “that one didn’t go through,” the user can instantly pull the last code back up without losing their place in the sequence.

**Problem it solves:** In rapid succession scanning, users need reassurance and recovery, not just speed.

## 8. Magic Wallet Surface

**Pitch:** On the morning of a trip, PassDrop could quietly promote one smart surface to the top of the app: a giant “Today: Efteling” card that opens straight into the family bundle and later morphs into “Leaving now? Parking ticket ready.” The delight comes from the app seeming almost uncannily prepared, like it remembered the plan and laid out the right cards before the user even asked. It makes grouped passes feel ceremonial and cared for, not just stored.

**Problem it solves:** Users should not have to mentally re-orient themselves every time they reopen the app during the same outing.
